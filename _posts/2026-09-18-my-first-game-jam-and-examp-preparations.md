Haven't posted anything for quite some time. In August, I put all my effort into preparing for an English exam (TOEFL), which took place in September. I've been thinking for a while about getting a master's degree at an Austrian university, so I decided to take this test. I wouldn't say the test itself was hard; I was just anxious about paying a fairly big price for it (approx. $300 just for one attempt) and not passing, so I decided to focus all my efforts on preparing for it. Luckily, I got an average score of 5.5 (roughly upper C1), which meets the university's requirements, so now I can apply for a degree (yay).

I barely did anything outside of test preparation for a month, so I was happy to sign up for a game jam on itch.io, specifically [Mini Jam 219: Nocturne](https://itch.io/jam/mini-jam-219-nocturne) in September. The mini jam takes only 3 days over a weekend, and as luck would have it, the jam overlapped with my TOEFL exam. I didn't submit the game, but I finished it anyway just for myself, working on it 1 day before and 2 days after the exam.

The theme of the game jam was "Nocturne" (as a non-native speaker, I still struggle to understand what it means), and the limitation was "Your score serves a purpose". As a game engine, I decided to use love2d (love it) and created a small progression game about a frog catching fireflies at night on a platform. It was fun: I love working with love2d and drawing pixel art sprites (though I really struggle with them), and the whole project consists of only 1068 lines of Lua code.
